### Yoga for Anxiety, Stress & Insomnia — Calm Built Into Your Week

A KayaSadhak teacher comes to your home in Delhi NCR to guide a practice built around calming an overactive nervous system — parasympathetic-focused breathing, Yoga Nidra for genuinely deep rest, and guided meditation, adapted to whether your main issue is daytime anxiety, trouble winding down, or both.

The mechanism here is fairly well understood: slow, guided breathing and stillness practices shift the body out of a stress response and into what's usually called "rest and digest" mode — lower heart rate, lower cortisol, and (for a lot of people) a genuinely easier time falling and staying asleep. It doesn't work instantly for everyone, and it isn't a substitute for treating a diagnosed anxiety disorder, but as a regular practice it's one of the more consistently useful tools available for everyday stress and sleep disruption.

### What a Session Includes

- **Parasympathetic breathwork:** slow breathing techniques specifically chosen to activate the body's calming response, useful both during a session and as something you can use on your own during a stressful moment
- **Yoga Nidra:** a guided, lying-down relaxation practice — often described as "yogic sleep" — that many people use specifically for insomnia, since it works with the body's relaxation response even on nights when falling asleep normally feels impossible
- **Guided meditation:** simple, accessible techniques (not requiring any prior meditation experience) aimed at quieting a racing mind rather than an advanced spiritual practice
- **Gentle movement:** where useful, slow, non-strenuous asana to release physical tension that often accompanies chronic stress — this isn't a workout-paced session

### Daytime Anxiety vs. Nighttime Insomnia — Different Emphasis

If your main struggle is daytime anxiety, sessions typically lean more toward breathwork you can use in the moment during a stressful day. If it's primarily insomnia, more time goes into Yoga Nidra and pre-sleep wind-down practices you can eventually do on your own before bed. Tell your teacher which is the bigger issue for you, since the emphasis genuinely shifts.

### A Note on What This Isn't

If you're managing a diagnosed anxiety disorder, panic attacks, or clinical insomnia, this practice is meant to sit alongside professional care, not replace it. If you have a trauma history, mention it when booking — some relaxation practices can bring up unexpected feelings for people with a trauma background, and your teacher can adapt accordingly.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Can yoga actually help with insomnia, or is that overstated?**
Practices like Yoga Nidra and slow breathwork are widely used and reported to help with sleep quality by calming an overactive nervous system, and there's a reasonable body of research supporting this. Results vary by person, and severe or long-standing insomnia should also be discussed with a doctor.

**Is Yoga Nidra the same as regular sleep?**
No — you stay lying down and follow a guided voice through a structured relaxation process; it's a distinct practice, and many people report feeling deeply rested afterward even without literally falling asleep during it.

**I have a diagnosed anxiety disorder — can this still help?**
Yes, many people use yoga alongside professional treatment for anxiety, but please continue any therapy or medication your doctor or therapist has recommended — this practice supports that care, it doesn't replace it.

**What if I fall asleep during a Yoga Nidra session?**
That's completely normal and not a problem — it usually just means your body needed the rest.

**Do I need any prior meditation experience?**
No. The meditation and breathwork techniques used are accessible to complete beginners.

**Can this help with panic attacks specifically?**
The breathing techniques taught can be genuinely useful in the moment during heightened anxiety, but if you experience panic attacks regularly, please also see a doctor or mental health professional for proper evaluation and treatment.
