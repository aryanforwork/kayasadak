### Therapeutic Yoga for Healthy Weight Management

A KayaSadhak teacher comes to your home in Delhi NCR to guide a yoga practice built around metabolic and core-focused work — Agnisar kriya and similar breath-and-abdominal techniques, core-strengthening asana, and a pace that actually fits into a real week, rather than a punishing routine you abandon after ten days.

Yoga isn't going to out-burn a bad diet, and we won't pretend otherwise. What it does reliably do is build the kind of consistent movement habit that's genuinely sustainable, strengthen the core and metabolism-supporting muscles a desk job quietly weakens, and — for a lot of people — reduce the stress-driven eating that no amount of willpower alone seems to fix. Combined with reasonable eating habits, that combination tends to work better long-term than an intense but short-lived gym phase.

### What This Practice Includes

- **Agnisar kriya:** a traditional breathing-and-abdominal technique that works the deep core through rhythmic contraction, used specifically to support digestion and metabolic activity — not a magic fat-burner, but a genuinely useful, low-impact core practice
- **Core-strengthening asana:** poses that build functional core strength beyond a typical crunch, supporting posture and overall tone
- **Metabolic-focused sequencing:** a flowing pace through the session, chosen because gentle continuous movement tends to be more sustainable than short bursts of intensity for most beginners
- **Stress-awareness component:** since stress-driven eating undermines weight goals for a lot of people, sessions include enough breathwork to address that side of things too, not just the physical

### A Few Honest Precautions

Agnisar kriya and similar breath-retention techniques aren't right for everyone — they're generally avoided by people with high blood pressure, heart disease, peptic ulcers, an overactive thyroid, or during pregnancy. Tell your teacher about any of these before your first session so they can build an appropriate practice for you instead.

### Realistic Expectations

Visible change from a metabolic-focused practice like this typically takes consistent weeks, not days — most people who stick with it notice a difference in energy and how their clothes fit before they see a big number change on a scale, and that's a genuinely normal order for things to happen in. Consistency matters more here than intensity.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Can yoga alone help me lose weight without changing my diet?**
Yoga supports metabolism, core strength, and stress management, all of which help — but diet plays a large role in weight management regardless of exercise type. Most people see the best results combining this practice with reasonably sensible eating, not through yoga alone.

**Is Agnisar kriya safe for everyone?**
No — it's generally avoided by people with high blood pressure, heart disease, peptic ulcers, an overactive thyroid, or during pregnancy. Tell your teacher about any of these conditions and they'll build a different practice for you.

**How is this different from a regular gym workout for weight loss?**
Yoga takes a gentler, more sustainable approach focused on consistency, core strength, and the stress side of weight management, rather than high-intensity calorie burn. Some people prefer combining both; others find yoga alone fits their life and body better.

**How long before I see results?**
This varies a great deal by individual, starting point, and consistency. Most people notice energy and fitness changes within a few weeks; visible weight change typically takes longer and depends heavily on diet alongside the practice.

**I have high blood pressure — can I still book this?**
Yes, but tell us when booking. Your teacher will build a modified practice that avoids the specific techniques not suited to high blood pressure, focusing instead on gentler core and movement work.

**Do I need to be already fit to start?**
No — most people starting a weight-management-focused practice are complete beginners. Your teacher starts at your current level and builds up gradually.
