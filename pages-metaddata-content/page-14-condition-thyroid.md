### Yoga for Thyroid Health — Hypothyroid & Hyperthyroid Support

A KayaSadhak teacher comes to your home in Delhi NCR to guide a yoga practice adapted specifically to whether you're managing hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid) or hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid) — because, importantly, these two conditions generally call for different, sometimes opposite, approaches to the neck-focused poses often associated with thyroid yoga.

Fatigue, weight changes, and low energy from hypothyroidism, or the restlessness and sensitivity to heat from hyperthyroidism, both respond differently to activity and stimulation — which is exactly why a teacher who knows your specific diagnosis matters more here than in many other areas of yoga.

### Hypothyroidism: A Generally Stimulating Approach

For hypothyroidism, gentle neck-stretching and thyroid-stimulating poses — supported shoulder stand, fish pose, and chin-lock (Jalandhara Bandha) variations — are commonly used, on the reasoning that increased blood flow to the neck and throat area may support thyroid function. Your teacher will use supported, beginner-friendly variations rather than the full advanced pose, especially early on.

### Hyperthyroidism: A Generally Calming, Non-Stimulating Approach

For hyperthyroidism, the same neck-stimulating poses are typically avoided or significantly modified, since further stimulating an already overactive gland isn't the goal. Instead, sessions lean toward calming, restorative poses and breathing techniques aimed at lowering overall nervous system activation.

### Breathing Practice: Ujjayi (Ocean Breath)

Ujjayi pranayama — a slow, audible breath created by gently contracting the throat — is commonly used in thyroid-focused yoga for both conditions, since it works the throat area gently through breath rather than through pose intensity.

### Why This Needs a Real Teacher, Not a Video

Hypothyroid and hyperthyroid poses are, in places, genuinely opposite approaches to the same body area — a generic "yoga for thyroid" video usually can't tell you which one applies to your situation, and doing the wrong one isn't just unhelpful, it can work against you. A home teacher who knows your specific diagnosis builds the session correctly from day one.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Can yoga cure my thyroid condition?**
No. Yoga is a complementary practice that some people find supports overall wellbeing alongside thyroid conditions, but it does not replace medication or medical treatment. Please continue any treatment your doctor has prescribed.

**Is shoulder stand safe for me?**
It depends on your specific condition and other health factors — shoulder stand and similar inversions aren't recommended for people with high blood pressure or certain cardiovascular issues regardless of thyroid status, and the pose is generally approached differently for hypo- vs. hyperthyroidism. Tell your teacher your full health picture before starting.

**I don't know if I have hypo- or hyperthyroidism — can I still book this?**
We'd recommend confirming your diagnosis with your doctor first, since the right approach genuinely differs between the two conditions. Once you know, we can build the right session for you.

**How long before I might notice a difference?**
This varies significantly by individual and isn't guaranteed — some people find the stress-reduction and general wellbeing benefits noticeable within a few weeks, while any effect on thyroid function itself should be monitored through your regular medical checkups, not by how a session feels.

**Can I do this alongside my thyroid medication?**
Yes, yoga is generally used alongside standard thyroid treatment, not instead of it. Continue your medication as prescribed and mention it to your teacher for context.

**What if I'm not sure which poses are right for me?**
That's exactly why a one-on-one teacher matters here — tell them your diagnosis and any other health details, and they'll build the session around what's actually appropriate for you.
