A session built around your actual stage, rather than a fixed studio-class curriculum, is the main reason people choose a home teacher over a group prenatal class.

### What This Covers, Trimester by Trimester

**First trimester:** gentle, closely guided movement — this is the most sensitive period developmentally, so sessions stay conservative and focus heavily on breathing and easing common early-pregnancy discomfort like nausea and fatigue.

**Second trimester:** as energy typically returns, sessions build in hip-opening and gentle strength work, with modifications as your center of gravity shifts.

**Third trimester:** poses and breathing techniques oriented toward labor preparation — pelvic floor work, hip mobility, and pranayama for managing contractions — alongside continued comfort-focused stretching for the final weeks.

**Postnatal:** once you have medical clearance, sessions focus on pelvic floor rehabilitation, core recovery (including diastasis recti awareness), and gentle strength rebuilding, at whatever pace your recovery calls for.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Is prenatal yoga safe in the first trimester?**
Gentle, appropriately modified yoga is commonly practiced through pregnancy, but the first trimester calls for extra caution given how sensitive early development is. We ask for your doctor's clearance before the first session and keep first-trimester practice deliberately conservative.

**Can prenatal yoga actually help with a normal (vaginal) delivery?**
Hip-opening poses, pelvic floor strengthening, and breathing techniques taught in later-trimester prenatal yoga are widely used to support labor preparation, but outcomes depend on many factors beyond yoga alone — please discuss your specific situation and goals with your doctor.

**When can I start postnatal yoga after delivery?**
This depends entirely on your delivery (normal vs. C-section) and your doctor's clearance — some gentle breathing work can often begin very early, but any physical postnatal practice should wait until you're medically cleared.

**Do you only send female teachers for this service?**
A female teacher is our default recommendation and is available on request; if you're comfortable with a male teacher, let us know when booking.

**What if I have a high-risk pregnancy?**
Please tell us upfront. We may still be able to help with very gentle, doctor-approved breathing work, but active postural practice may not be appropriate — we'll always be honest with you about this rather than book a session that isn't right for your situation.

**How is postnatal yoga different from a general fitness plan after delivery?**
Postnatal yoga specifically prioritizes pelvic floor rehabilitation and core recovery (including checking for diastasis recti) before building general strength — starting general fitness work too early or in the wrong order can slow recovery.
